# Weekly Journal 9

## Exploration & Experimentation

This week I got feedback about my journal (positive, yayy mee), and I also got the very useful suggestion to narrow down my documentation so it clearly shows a path toward the final piece. So I sat with that and decided: I want a moon/sun based theme. I want the two celestial bodies to mirror the shadow and light sides every human carries. Because yes, I’m emotional, and yes, I am using code to process that.

I did small direction-setting experiments rather than big finished sketches. I tested warm vs cool palettes, tried simple state switching, and started thinking about what kind of interaction would feel right. Clicking to toggle felt too harsh. I wanted something more like turning, shifting, cycling, like time moving whether I want it to or not.

[Image: Warm/cool palette studies and early “two-mode” tests.]

## Influences & References
My reference this week was more conceptual: symbolism and cycles. Sun and moon are ancient visual metaphors for change, return, and duality. That matters because it means the theme is readable even if the viewer doesn’t know my personal story. And honestly, making something readable matters when you’re presenting and explaining a system. This checkpoint also made critique itself feel like part of the artwork’s development, my “machine” isn’t just code; it’s also my decisions reacting to feedback.

## Algorithmic Thinking
The algorithmic structure in my head became a state machine. If I define a variable like mode or a continuous parameter like t from 0 to 1, then everything can depend on it: background color, star density, face expression, and detail elements. The rule becomes: one variable controls many outputs. That is exactly the kind of system that makes generative work feel cohesive instead of random.

## Critical Reflection
What worked: I finally committed to a direction instead of bouncing between pretty experiments. What failed: I didn’t yet have the perfect transition mechanic. I had “switches,” but I didn’t have “flow.” Next step: find a transition that feels natural and physical, rotation keeps calling to me, because it literally embodies cycles.
